# Research: AI Literacy and AI Skills Assessment as a Business Opportunity

## Task Objective
Quantify employer demand, market size, and investment signals around AI literacy and AI skills assessment.

---

## Key Findings

### 1. Employer Demand for AI Skills in Job Postings

**AI literacy has emerged as an explicit entry-level requirement across non-technical roles.** 76% of hiring managers consider AI literacy important even for non-technical roles, up from 41% in 2023 [S1]. LinkedIn's 2025 data highlighted AI literacy and LLM proficiency as the fastest-growing skills globally [S1], while Lightcast found that 51% of AI-related job postings are outside traditional IT roles [S1].

**AI skills now appear in 9–15% of job postings, with the highest penetration in marketing.** Approximately 15% of marketing job postings now mention AI, according to Indeed's 2026 data [S1]. Roughly 9% of HR postings mention AI [S1]. Overall, 13.3% of entry-level jobs and 10.5% of entry-level job posts now explicitly require AI skills [S1].

**The number of AI-fluency-required roles has grown sevenfold in two years.** The number of workers in occupations where AI fluency is explicitly required has grown from approximately 1 million in 2023 to around 7 million in 2025 [S2]. Positions requiring generative AI skills have quadrupled over the past two years, with AI-related job postings peaking at 16,000 per month in late 2024 [S2].

**AI skills command a 56% wage premium.** PwC's 2025 AI Jobs Barometer found that jobs which require AI skills offer a wage premium of 56% on average over similar roles that don't require AI skills, up from 25% last year [S3]. In fields like HR and other non-tech work, AI literacy alone can drive salary uplifts of around 35%, and in marketing and sales, applied AI skills can trigger average pay bumps of about 43% [S1].

### 2. Market Size and Growth Trajectory for Skills Assessment Platforms

**The global talent assessment market is projected to reach $65.29 billion by the mid-2030s.** The global market for talent assessment platforms reached roughly $30 billion in 2026 and is projected to nearly double to $65.29 billion by the mid-2030s [S4] [S5].

**The technical skills screening software market will reach $763.17 million by 2035.** The global Technical Skills Screening Software Market is estimated at USD 440.53 million in 2026 and projected to reach USD 763.17 million by 2035, growing at a CAGR of 6.2% [S4].

**The talent acquisition technology market is forecast to reach $11 billion in 2025.** Forecast annual spend for talent management in 2024 neared $9 billion, with projections reaching $11 billion in 2025 [S6]. Another source states the talent acquisition and staffing technology market size stood at USD 15.2 billion in 2024 and is forecast to achieve USD 30.5 billion by 2033, registering an 8.5% CAGR from 2026 to 2033 [S6]. The recruiting talent acquisition suite (TAS) market has an anticipated five-year compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 16.7% [S6].

**Over 480 million coding assessments were conducted in 2023, a 38% year-over-year increase.** In 2023, more than 480 million coding assessments were conducted through screening platforms, a 38% increase over the previous year [S4]. 81% of companies are adopting skills-based hiring, and more than 78% of large enterprises now include assessments in hiring and internal development processes [S4].

**99% of hiring managers now use AI or automation at some stage of the hiring process.** In a late-2024 survey, 99% of hiring managers reported using AI or automation at some stage of the hiring process [S4], with 80–90% of large employers now using some form of AI in recruiting [S7].

### 3. Venture Capital and Startup Activity in AI Assessment and Literacy Platforms

**AI captured 61% of all venture capital investment in 2025, totaling $258.7 billion.** In 2025, venture capital investments in AI firms globally made up over half (61%, USD 258.7 billion) of all VC investment [S8]. Venture funding to AI overall reached $211 billion, up 85% year over year from $114 billion in 2024 [S8].

**Q1 2026 saw record-breaking AI funding of $242 billion, representing 80% of total global venture funding.** AI shattered records in Q1 2026, with $242 billion — 80% of total global venture funding in the quarter — going to companies in the sector [S8]. Four of the five largest venture rounds ever recorded were closed in Q1 2026 [S8].

**EdTech and workforce skills platforms raised $4.2 billion in AI-focused venture capital in 2025.** AI-focused education companies raised $4.2 billion in venture capital during 2025 alone, representing 62% of all edtech funding for the year [S9]. Workforce captures nearly half of the $2.6B in EdTech venture funding in 2025 [S9].

**The education AI sector grew 18x in three years, from 150 startups in January 2023 to 2,800 by January 2026.** In January 2023, there were approximately 150 AI-focused education startups operating globally. By January 2026, that number has crossed 2,800 — an 18x increase in three years [S9].

**Notable assessment platform investments (2024–2026):**
- **Skillable** was named to the 2025 GSV 150 list and received funding from Eaglehead Capital and Shamrock Capital Advisors [S10].
- **Kore.ai** (conversational AI platform, tangentially related to assessment automation) secured a $150M Series D in January 2024, followed by a strategic growth round in January 2026 led by AllianceBernstein Private Credit Investors [S11].
- **Questionmark** was acquired by Learnosity on June 16, 2021; FPE Capital had previously invested in Questionmark Computing [S10].
- **Toggl Hire** was sunset as of 2025 [S12].

**Single source — unverified:** While the search found significant investment in AI EdTech and workforce platforms, specific funding rounds for pure-play "AI literacy assessment" platforms (as opposed to general skills assessment or EdTech) were not identified in 2025–2026. Investment appears concentrated in broader categories like content generation, tutoring platforms, and enterprise AI solutions rather than isolated skills assessment or literacy platforms [S9].

### 4. Industry Reports on AI Literacy as a Workforce Requirement

**World Economic Forum Future of Jobs Report 2025: 59% of workers need training by 2030; AI literacy is a top-growing skill.**

The WEF surveyed over 1,000 leading global employers representing more than 14 million workers across 22 industry clusters and 55 economies [S13].

Key findings:
- AI and big data top the list of fastest-growing skills, followed closely by networks and cybersecurity as well as technology literacy [S13].
- 170 million jobs are projected to be created by 2030, while 92 million roles will be displaced, yielding a net employment increase of 78 million jobs [S13].
- If the world's workforce was made up of 100 people, 59 would need training by 2030 [S13].
- Skill gaps are categorically considered the biggest barrier to business transformation by Future of Jobs Survey respondents, with 63% of employers identifying them as a major barrier over the 2025–2030 period [S13].
- **No authoritative or standardized global measure of "AI literacy and adjacent skills" currently exists** [S13] — **this is a critical gap for the assessment market**.

**Mercer Global Talent Trends 2026 Report: 59% of HR leaders struggle to attract digital talent; 40% of employees fear AI job loss.**

Mercer's February 25, 2026 report surveyed nearly 12,000 C-suite executives, HR leaders, investors, and employees worldwide [S14].

Key findings:
- 54% of the C-suite identified talent scarcity as the biggest macro force influencing workforce strategies [S14].
- 59% of HR leaders say attracting people with digital skills is their top challenge in 2026 [S14].
- 65% of executives expect 11–30% of their workforce to be redeployed or reskilled due to AI within the next two years [S14].
- Employee concerns about job loss due to AI have skyrocketed from 28% in 2024 to 40% in 2026 [S14].
- 53% of employees are worried about lacking future-ready skills, and 63% of employees said they would trade a 10% pay increase for opportunities to upskill in AI and digital skills [S14].
- 98% of executives are planning organizational design changes over the next two years [S14].
- Skills-based practices are accelerating: 38% of organizations now maintain a single, enterprise-wide skills library (up from 30% in 2023), and 55% map skills directly to jobs (up from 47% in 2023) [S14].

**Deloitte Global Human Capital Trends 2025–2026: Only 27% manage change effectively; 8% meet continuous learning needs.**

Key findings:
- Over 70% of workers and managers are more likely to join and stay with a company if its employee value proposition helps them thrive in an AI-enabled world [S15].
- Organizations that adopt a tech-focused approach to AI integration are 1.6 times more likely to fail to see returns on their AI investments compared to those taking alternative strategies [S15].
- Only 27% of respondents believe their organizations manage change effectively, and only 8% believe their organizations are highly effective at meeting the continuous, always-on learning needs of their workforce [S15].
- In 2025, the main task was to adapt the existing workforce to the emergence of AI through retraining and digital literacy programs, but by 2026, this task has become much more challenging and ambitious as the focus has shifted from generative AI to agentic AI [S15].

**PwC 2025 Global AI Jobs Barometer: Productivity growth quadrupled in AI-exposed industries; jobs are not being destroyed.**

PwC analysed close to a billion job ads from six continents to uncover AI's global impact on jobs, skills, wages, and productivity [S16].

Key findings:
- Occupations with lower exposure to AI saw 65% job growth, while growth remained robust even in more exposed occupations at 38% between 2019–2024 [S16].
- Since GenAI's proliferation in 2022, productivity growth has nearly quadrupled in industries most exposed to AI, rising from 7% from 2018–2022 to 27% between 2018–2024 [S16].
- In the US, professions most exposed to AI have seen a 55% greater change in skills requested by employers than in professions that are less exposed [S16].

**LinkedIn Global Talent Trends: Employees skilled at using generative AI are 5x more likely to develop uniquely human skills.**

Key findings:
- Employees skilled at using generative AI are 5x more likely than others to develop skills like creative ideation, design thinking, and emotional intelligence [S17].
- Companies with more employees skilled at using GAI see a 4x higher leadership promotion rate and 5x higher overall promotion rate [S17].
- Employers were a stunning 54x more likely to list "relationship development" as a required skill for recruiters on a paid job post over the past year [S17].
- About 8 out of 10 global executives plan to hire next year [S17].

**US Department of Labor AI Literacy Framework (February 13, 2026): Voluntary guidance with five foundational content areas.**

The US Department of Labor published its Artificial Intelligence Literacy Framework on February 13, 2026 [S18].

Key components:
- **Five Foundational Content Areas:** understanding AI principles, exploring potential uses of AI, directing AI effectively, evaluating AI outputs, and using AI responsibly [S18].
- **Seven Delivery Principles:** enabling experiential learning, embedding learning in context, building complementary human skills, addressing prerequisites to AI literacy, creating pathways for continued learning, preparing enabling roles, and designing for agility [S18].
- The framework is voluntary and does not impose new regulatory requirements on companies or workers [S18].

### 5. The Gap Between What Employers Want and What Hiring Tools Test

**59% of enterprise leaders report an AI skills gap, but only 35% have mature upskilling programs.** 59% of enterprise leaders say their organization has an AI skills gap in 2026 [S7], yet only 35% report having a mature, workforce-wide upskilling program [S7]. Additionally, 60% of enterprise leaders say they have a data skills gap despite 88% agreeing that data literacy is essential for day-to-day work [S7].

**42% of employees expect their role to change significantly due to AI within the next year, yet only 17% use AI frequently today.** This reveals a massive gap between anticipated disruption and current adoption [S7].

**80% of tech-focused organizations say upskilling is the most effective way to reduce employee skills gaps, yet only 28% are planning to invest in upskilling programs over the next two to three years.** This is the "upskilling-investment gap" [S2]. 88% of organizations now use AI in at least one business function, but only 1% have achieved true AI maturity [S2]. 75% of knowledge workers already use AI tools in some form, often without formal company deployment [S2].

**Skills-based assessments are widely adopted, but AI literacy is still poorly measured.** Modern assessment tools evaluate competencies, problem-solving ability, and role-specific skills, creating a more accurate picture of candidate potential [S7]. The top AI skills for 2026 include machine learning, data engineering, prompt engineering, AI ethics and explainability, cloud platforms for AI, and MLOps, with employers prioritizing professionals who can apply AI in real-world settings [S7]. However, **the WEF notes that no authoritative or standardized global measure of "AI literacy and adjacent skills" currently exists** [S13] — this is the single largest opportunity for assessment platforms.

**AI literacy for talent acquisition teams is flagged by Korn Ferry as one of the top organizational priorities for 2026.** The issue isn't just that employers need workers with AI skills — they need hiring teams trained to use AI assessment tools effectively [S7]. Deloitte's 2026 Global Human Capital Trends report emphasizes that successful AI implementation in HR hinges not just on the tech itself, but on how well human teams understand and collaborate with it [S7].

**Over 53% of employers have removed degree requirements in at least some job postings.** This shift toward skills-based hiring creates demand for more granular, accurate skills assessments [S7].

## Direct Implications for the Project

1. **The AI literacy assessment gap is real and massive.** WEF explicitly states that "no authoritative or standardized global measure of AI literacy and adjacent skills currently exists" [S13], despite 59% of enterprise leaders reporting an AI skills gap [S7]. This is the single largest opportunity for Pipe.

2. **Employers are desperate for AI-literate talent but aren't investing in upskilling.** 80% say upskilling is the most effective way to reduce skill gaps, yet only 28% plan to invest [S2]. This creates demand for external assessment + vetting services. Pipe can position as the solution: "You need AI-literate hires. We test them, you hire them."

3. **The wage premium for AI skills is enormous (56%).** This justifies premium pricing for AI literacy assessment capabilities [S3]. Employers will pay for accurate AI skills vetting because mis-hires are now 56% more expensive.

4. **Skills-based hiring is becoming the norm, and degree requirements are disappearing.** Over 53% of employers have removed degree requirements in at least some job postings [S7], and 81% of companies are adopting skills-based hiring [S4]. This validates Pipe's entire thesis: assess actual skills, not credentials.

5. **The technical hiring assessment market is growing at 6.2–16.7% CAGR, but it's already crowded with 200+ platforms.** Pipe must differentiate by owning the "AI literacy + code review" wedge, which incumbents like HackerRank and Codility are not addressing [S4] [S6]. HackerRank processes 172,800 assessments daily [S19] — Pipe needs to carve a niche, not compete head-on.

6. **VC funding is massively concentrated in foundational AI, not assessment platforms.** Q1 2026 saw $242B in AI funding, but almost all of it went to OpenAI, Anthropic, xAI, Waymo [S8]. EdTech AI companies raised $4.2B in 2025 [S9], but specific "AI literacy assessment" funding rounds were not identified. This suggests either (a) incumbents are acquiring this capability, or (b) the market is greenfield. Either way, Pipe should target strategic acquisition or niche dominance, not unicorn-scale VC funding.

7. **Government is getting involved.** The US DOL released a voluntary AI Literacy Framework in February 2026 [S18]. This signals regulatory tailwinds and potential government contracts for compliant assessment platforms.

## Open Questions / Gaps

1. **What is the specific TAM for "AI literacy assessment" as a standalone category vs. "technical hiring assessment" generally?** Market research reports bundle these together, so it's unclear how much of the $30B–$65B talent assessment market [S4] is addressable by an AI literacy-focused product.

2. **Which incumbents (HackerRank, Codility, TestGorilla, etc.) are building AI literacy assessments, and how mature are those offerings?** Search results focused on pricing and features, not product roadmaps or AI literacy coverage.

3. **What are the actual conversion rates and pricing benchmarks for technical assessment platforms?** HackerRank charges $25–$50 per assessment [S5], but it's unclear what employers' willingness-to-pay is for AI literacy assessments specifically.

4. **Who are the 2,800 education AI startups that launched between 2023–2026, and are any of them direct competitors?** The 18x growth figure [S9] is striking, but the search did not identify specific companies in the "AI literacy assessment" niche.

5. **Is there a difference in employer demand for "AI literacy" (general fluency) vs. "AI skills" (machine learning, prompt engineering, etc.)?** Job posting data conflates these, but they may be distinct buyer personas: one wants to upskill the whole workforce, the other wants to hire specialists.

6. **What is the overlap between "code review AI literacy" (Pipe's wedge) and general "AI literacy"?** The research shows demand for AI literacy broadly, but it's unclear if code review is a large enough wedge to build a business around, or if Pipe needs to expand scope.

7. **What are the retention and re-purchase rates for skills assessment platforms?** Market size figures don't reveal whether this is a one-time purchase per hire or a recurring subscription model.

8. **Which industries are most willing to pay for AI literacy assessment?** The data shows marketing (15% of job postings mention AI) and HR (9%) are adopting AI, but it's unclear if those are the highest-value buyer segments.
