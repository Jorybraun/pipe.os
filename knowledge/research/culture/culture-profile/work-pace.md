# Work Pace

**Type:** culture-profile dimension
**Scale:** 1-5 slider
**Evidence required:** verbatim quotes

---

## What this slider captures

How the candidate prefers to structure their time and attention across a working day or week. This is about rhythm, not output volume — a steady worker and a fast worker can produce the same amount.

---

## Anchors

- **1 — Steady.** Prefers deep focus blocks, sustainable weekly rhythm, low context switching. Describes working best with fewer interruptions. Protects calendar carefully.
- **2 — Mostly steady.** Accommodates occasional sprints but returns to steady rhythm.
- **3 — Balanced.** Comfortable in both modes. Describes shifting pace to match project phase.
- **4 — Fast paced.** Comfortable with multiple simultaneous contexts, frequent context switching, reactive work alongside planned work.
- **5 — High pace.** Thrives on a reactive, high-interrupt environment. Describes enjoying crunch periods. Multiple simultaneous projects is the preferred state, not an exception.

---

## Signal sources

- **Low:** language around "focus time," "blocking calendar," "deep work," preference for asynchronous communication
- **High:** language around "juggling," "sprinting," "crisis response," preference for real-time communication

Note the interaction with burnout risk: a candidate who describes working at pace 5 for an extended period may be heading into burnout regardless of their stated comfort. The scoring agent should flag this pattern in the narrative rather than in the slider itself.
