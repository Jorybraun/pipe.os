# Feedback Orientation

**Type:** culture-profile dimension
**Scale:** 1-5 slider
**Evidence required:** verbatim quotes

---

## What this slider captures

How the candidate prefers feedback to be given and received in a working environment. This is one of the most common sources of daily friction between individuals and teams — a mismatch here causes ongoing discomfort regardless of how competent everyone is.

---

## Anchors

- **1 — Diplomatic / structured.** Prefers private, scheduled feedback with time to reflect. Uncomfortable with public criticism even when constructive. Prefers structured review cycles over ad-hoc comments.
- **2 — Measured.** Mostly private, some real-time. Prefers feedback delivered with care and context.
- **3 — Balanced.** Adapts to the situation. Comfortable giving and receiving feedback in multiple modes.
- **4 — Direct.** Comfortable with candid, real-time feedback. Prefers directness over diplomacy when they conflict.
- **5 — Radically direct.** Values bluntness, real-time public feedback, strong priors on honesty-over-comfort. Describes past environments where feedback was "brutal" positively.

---

## Signal sources

- **Low:** language around "setting," "framing," "making sure they're ready," structured review processes
- **High:** language around "calling out," "being direct," "not sugar-coating," public channels for feedback

A common asymmetry: candidates often prefer to *receive* feedback diplomatically and *give* it directly. The scoring agent should report both directions separately if evidence supports it.

This dimension interacts with conflict handling (competency) and is one of the most legally sensitive to misread — direct feedback cultures can feel hostile to people from backgrounds where that style reads as disrespect. The recruiter report must never frame "high diplomatic preference" as a weakness.
