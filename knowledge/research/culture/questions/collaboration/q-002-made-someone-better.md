## Question

Tell me about a specific time you helped someone on your team become more effective at their work. What did you do, and how did you know it worked?

## Why we ask this

The collaboration question that separates multipliers from merely-pleasant coworkers. A strong collaborator can name specific people they made better and describe the evidence. A weak collaborator describes "helping the team" in aggregate without specifics.

This also surfaces candidates who conflate "telling people what to do" with helping them grow. The probe should push on whether the help made the person more autonomous over time.

## BARS rubric

- **5** — Names a specific person, describes the gap or friction the person had, describes a deliberate intervention (pairing, feedback, a process change, a handoff), and describes evidence the person became more effective — ideally including that the person later did something independently that they previously would have needed help with. The candidate's role is clear but the growth belongs to the other person.
- **4** — Specific person and intervention. Good evidence of improvement. Slightly less clarity on autonomy-building.
- **3** — Describes helping in general terms or a one-time help. Evidence of impact is vague.
- **2** — Describes "helping the team" with no specific person. Or: describes telling someone what to do once without growth evidence.
- **1** — Cannot name a specific person. Or: frames their help as fixing the other person's work themselves. Or: claims credit for the other person's growth in a way that hides the other person's agency.

## Calibration examples

### Low (1-2)

> "I try to help everyone on my team. I do a lot of code review and answer questions in our team channel whenever someone gets stuck."

No specific person, no specific intervention, no evidence. Scores 1.

### Medium (3)

> "I paired with a new grad on our team for a few weeks to get her up to speed. We went through a few tickets together and she started handling them on her own after that."

Specific person, clear intervention, implicit evidence, but no detail on what she specifically grew into. Scores 3.

### High (4-5)

> "A mid-level engineer on my team had a habit of asking me to review his design docs before he'd share them with anyone else — he was worried about looking wrong in front of the team. I started declining the pre-reviews. Instead I'd go to the design review meetings and ask him clarifying questions in front of everyone, so he'd get practice defending his own thinking in public. The first couple were shaky. By the fourth one he was answering questions I hadn't asked and pushing back on my own suggestions. Six months later he was running design reviews for his own projects. I told him what I'd been doing about a year in and he said he'd figured it out and appreciated it. What I know worked: he hasn't asked me for a pre-review since."

Specific person, specific friction (confidence in public), deliberate counter-intuitive intervention, clear evidence of autonomy growth, long-term follow-through. Scores 5.

## Probe library

- *no specific person*: "Can you pick one specific person and walk me through what you did with them?"
- *one-time help*: "Did the thing you helped with come up again later? What did they do the second time?"
- *fixing their work*: "Did you end up doing parts of the work yourself? How did you balance helping with letting them figure it out?"
- *vague impact*: "What did they do later that told you the help worked?"
