## Question

How do you think you come across to people who don't know you well yet? Is that how you'd want to come across, and what's the gap if there is one?

## Why we ask this

This is a non-STAR question — it's asking for self-reflection rather than a past example. It's included because self-awareness is the one dimension where past-behavior questions are weakest; candidates can describe self-awareness behaviors without actually being self-aware.

The gap question is the part that does the work. "How I come across" vs. "how I'd want to come across" — candidates with real self-awareness can name a gap. Candidates without it claim there's no gap, or describe a gap that's humblebrag ("I come across as too intense because I care too much").

## BARS rubric

- **5** — Names a specific, honest answer to how they come across — including details that aren't flattering. Names a real gap between that and how they'd want to come across. Includes at least one piece of evidence — a time someone told them, a pattern they've noticed in first-week interactions, something they learned in a 360 review. The gap is specific enough to act on.
- **4** — Real answer, honest, specific gap, some evidence.
- **3** — Real answer but the gap is mild or flattering. Some self-awareness but not very probing.
- **2** — Humblebrag weakness ("I can come across as intimidating because I'm very direct"). No real gap.
- **1** — Cannot answer. Or: describes how they come across in purely positive terms with no gap. Or: deflects into "I think I come across differently to different people, it depends."

## Calibration examples

### Low (1-2)

> "I think I come across as focused and driven. Sometimes maybe too intense? But I think people generally appreciate that I care about the work."

Humblebrag, no gap, no evidence. Scores 1.

### Medium (3)

> "I've been told I can come across as a bit quiet or reserved in my first few weeks on a team. I'd like to come across as more engaged, so I've been trying to speak up more in early meetings."

Honest, specific, mild gap, behavioral response. Scores 3.

### High (4-5)

> "I come across as more confident than I actually am in the first month or two. I speak quickly, I make strong claims in meetings, and I don't ask many clarifying questions early on because I'm trying to absorb context without signaling ignorance. What I've learned — from three different people over my career, including someone I worked with really closely at my last job — is that this reads to some people as dismissive or as already-having-opinions before I've earned them. That's not what I want. The gap is that I'd rather come across as curious and provisional in the first couple of months, because that's actually how I feel. I've started deliberately asking basic questions in my first few meetings on a new team, even when I could probably figure them out on my own. It still feels exposing but the feedback has been much better."

Specific, honest-about-unflattering, names sources of evidence (multiple people), real gap, concrete corrective action, acknowledgment that it's still hard. Scores 5.

## Probe library

- *humblebrag*: "That one can be a strength. Is there something that's more genuinely not how you'd want to come across?"
- *no gap*: "Is there any gap at all? Even a small one?"
- *no evidence*: "How do you know that's how you come across? What told you?"
- *deflects into situational*: "Pick one of the contexts — how do you come across there?"
