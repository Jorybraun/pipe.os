# Autonomy

**Type:** culture-profile dimension
**Scale:** 1-5 slider (both org benchmark and candidate inference)
**Evidence required:** verbatim quotes

---

## What this slider captures

How much structure the person needs or wants in their day-to-day work. Low autonomy does not mean "bad" — many people thrive with clear direction and regular check-ins. High autonomy does not mean "good" — self-directed people can drift without a strong frame.

The org benchmark is set by the recruiter based on what the actual team environment is. The candidate's position is inferred from transcript signals. The report shows both on the same axis, and a divergence of 2 or more is flagged for recruiter attention.

---

## Anchors

- **1 — Needs structure.** Candidate thrives with clear tasks, explicit priorities, and frequent check-ins. Asks for guidance when uncertain. Prefers well-defined ownership boundaries. Not reducible to "junior" — many senior ICs prefer this.
- **2 — Prefers structure.** Comfortable with some ambiguity but wants clear priorities at the sprint/week level.
- **3 — Balanced.** Adapts to both structured and ambiguous environments. Makes autonomy decisions based on context.
- **4 — Prefers autonomy.** Comfortable setting their own priorities. Describes past work where they decided what to work on.
- **5 — Needs autonomy.** Describes chafing under detailed process. Past work includes self-initiated projects that were not assigned. Uses language like "I saw X was broken so I just fixed it."

---

## Signal sources

Look for transcript evidence of:
- **Low:** candidate describes asking for direction, seeks confirmation, frames uncertainty as a problem to escalate
- **High:** candidate describes starting projects without being told to, deciding scope, pushing back on assigned work

Beware performative signals — "I love ambiguity" is common but weak. Look for concrete past actions, not stated preferences.
