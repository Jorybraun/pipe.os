# Risk Tolerance

**Type:** culture-profile dimension
**Scale:** 1-5 slider
**Evidence required:** verbatim quotes

---

## What this slider captures

How comfortable the candidate is with uncertainty, reversible failure, and shipping things that are not yet proven. This is about their working comfort zone, not their skill level.

---

## Anchors

- **1 — Cautious.** Prefers thorough testing, staged rollouts, careful review cycles. Past failures described with heavy emphasis on prevention. Discomfort with anything that feels rushed.
- **2 — Measured.** Comfortable with managed risk. Wants guardrails but will ship imperfect things if the guardrails exist.
- **3 — Balanced.** Adjusts risk posture to context. Ships fast when cheap to fix, slow when not.
- **4 — Ship-first.** Comfortable shipping early and iterating. Past failures described as learning events without excess anxiety.
- **5 — High tolerance.** Bias toward action, comfort with public failure, describes "moving fast and breaking things" positively. Past work includes shipping something that broke and fixing it in public.

---

## Signal sources

- **Low:** language around "making sure," "validating," "getting sign-off," extended descriptions of review processes
- **High:** language around "shipping," "iterating," "learning from production," acceptance of reversible mistakes

A candidate can be high on risk tolerance for product decisions and low for infrastructure or security decisions — these are different surfaces. The scoring agent should pick the dominant signal and flag the asymmetry in the evidence quotes.
