## Question

Tell me about feedback you've received at work that surprised you. What was it, who gave it to you, and how did you respond?

## Why we ask this

"Surprising" feedback is, by definition, feedback that revealed a blind spot. How a candidate describes it reveals a lot: do they still feel defensive about it years later, or have they integrated it? Do they describe the feedback as wrong, overstated, or accurate? Do they remember who gave it to them, and do they still have a good relationship with that person?

This is the shortest question in the bank but one of the most efficient at sorting.

## BARS rubric

- **5** — Specific feedback, specific giver, clearly remembered details. Describes the feedback accurately — not as "they thought I was X" but with the actual behavior that was flagged. Describes their initial reaction honestly (often including defensiveness), and what they did with the feedback over time. Either integrated the feedback with visible change, or made a principled decision to reject part of it with reasons.
- **4** — Specific, clear details, honest about initial reaction, good outcome.
- **3** — Specific feedback but the description of it is vague or softened. Decent response.
- **2** — Describes feedback that wasn't really surprising — just uncomfortable. Or: describes integrating feedback that was clearly correct ("they said I needed to document better, and they were right").
- **1** — Cannot remember any surprising feedback. Or: describes receiving feedback and deciding the giver was wrong without explanation. Or: describes feedback so old it no longer reflects current self.

## Calibration examples

### Low (1-2)

> "I got some feedback early in my career about documentation — my manager at the time thought I wasn't writing things down enough. In retrospect he was right, and I've been more disciplined about docs since then."

Old, widely-applicable, not actually surprising, and has the "obviously-right-in-retrospect" framing. Scores 2.

### Medium (3)

> "A peer once told me that in code review I sometimes came across as harsh. I was surprised because I didn't think I was, but I took it seriously and tried to soften my language. I started using more 'we could' framing instead of 'this is wrong.'"

Specific, specific source, behavioral response. Decent but thin on the initial reaction and the longer-term integration. Scores 3.

### High (4-5)

> "About eighteen months ago my tech lead told me that the juniors on our team were afraid to ping me for help. I was genuinely shocked because I thought I was really approachable — I'd made a point of being available in our team channel. He told me that the issue wasn't availability, it was that my answers were always *complete* — if someone asked a small question I'd give them the whole architectural context. The juniors found it overwhelming and had started going to a more junior engineer for help even though they knew I was the authority on the area. My first reaction was defensive — I thought, 'but that context is useful.' I stewed on it for a day and then realized the evidence was clear: the behavior I thought was helpful was in fact pushing people away. I started giving short answers and explicitly asking 'do you want the longer context or is that enough?' About a month in, two of the juniors actually started coming to me. One of them told me later my answers were easier to use now. I still have to catch myself — the urge to dump context is strong. I'd say that one piece of feedback reshaped how I mentor more than anything else in my career."

Specific, honest initial defensive reaction, clear evidence that made them take it seriously, durable behavior change, honest about residual struggle, meta-awareness of the feedback's impact. Scores 5.

## Probe library

- *old / widely applicable*: "That's a common one. Is there a piece of feedback that felt more specific to you?"
- *no initial reaction*: "What was your first reaction when you heard it? Was there any defensiveness?"
- *wasn't really surprising*: "Was it actually surprising, or uncomfortable? I'm looking for something you hadn't seen coming."
- *no behavior change*: "What did you actually do with it? Is there something you do differently now because of it?"
