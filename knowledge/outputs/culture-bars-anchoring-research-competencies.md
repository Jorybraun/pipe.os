## Summary

**What is well-sourced:** Three of the five competencies — Ownership & Initiative, Collaboration, and Conflict Handling — have strong multi-source anchor coverage from at least two independently derived frameworks each, with direct BARS-style level language. Korn Ferry Leadership Architect (KFLA, 38-competency library) is the single richest primary source: the standard competency library PDF was successfully extracted and yields 3-level (Unsuccessful / Successful / Exceptional) observable-behavior bullet lists for directly corresponding competencies (Ensures Accountability, Action Oriented, Collaborates, Manages Conflict, Demonstrates Self-Awareness, Nimble Learning, Self-Development). This is augmented by Valdosta State University's published 5-level BARS guide (Unsuccessful → Emerging → Successful → Distinguished → Exemplary), UC Berkeley's 5-level behavioral anchors matrix, and AIHR's practitioner BARS examples. The Goleman/Boyatzis Emotional Competence Inventory framework (peer-reviewed, Boyatzis et al. 2000) supplies validated competency language for Self-Awareness and Conflict Management, and Hogan HPI subscale documentation provides personality-rooted behavioral language for all five competencies.

**Partial coverage:** Learning Orientation and Self-Awareness have good framework coverage (KFLA "Nimble Learning" + "Self-Development" + "Demonstrates Self-Awareness"; Hogan Learning Approach and Inquisitive scales; Goleman Accurate Self-Assessment) but lack a single peer-reviewed BARS validation study with numbered level anchors specific to these constructs. The CCL learning agility research PDF did not yield readable text; the DTIC BARS document was 403-blocked.

**Gaps:** No peer-reviewed IO psychology journal article was found that provides a published, validated 5-point BARS instrument with numbered anchor text specifically for any of these five competencies in a work context. Most published BARS studies use clinical or military settings (e.g., the PMC medical simulation study, Krippendorff's α = 0.25–0.53 range). The Bartram Great Eight (2005) provides criterion-centric names and sub-competency descriptions but not level-by-level observable-behavior anchors. All "level 1–5" anchor text in this document is drawn from validated practitioner frameworks (KFLA, VSU, UC Berkeley) or translated from the KFLA 3-level system — clearly marked where translation is applied.

## Competency 1: Ownership & Initiative

### PIPE's working definition

Candidate takes personal responsibility for outcomes including those outside formal scope; stays with a problem past the handoff point; owns failures without deflecting; distinguishes themselves from passive "waiting for assignment" behavior.

### Frameworks that define it

1. **Korn Ferry Leadership Architect (KFLA) — "Ensures Accountability" (#1) and "Action Oriented" (#2)** [S1]
   Ensures Accountability: "Holding self and others accountable to meet commitments." Action Oriented: "Taking on new opportunities and tough challenges with a sense of urgency, high energy, and enthusiasm." These are two separate KFLA competencies that together map precisely to PIPE's Ownership definition: accountability-without-prompting (Ensures Accountability) and initiative-taking before being forced (Action Oriented).

2. **Goleman/Boyatzis Emotional Competence Inventory — "Initiative" and "Conscientiousness"** [S5]
   Goleman (2001, *The Emotionally Intelligent Workplace*): "Those with the Initiative competence act before being forced to do so by external events. This often means taking anticipatory action to avoid problems before they happen or taking advantage of opportunities before they are visible to anyone else. Individuals who lack Initiative are reactive rather than proactive." Conscientiousness "distinguishes the model organizational citizens."

3. **Hogan HPI — Prudence scale / Ambition scale** [S8]
   Prudence subscales (Mastery: "Concerned with doing a good job" vs. "Relaxed attitude about his/her work"; Avoids Trouble: "Considers actions and their consequences" vs. "Takes unnecessary and negative risks"). Ambition scale: Competitive, Accomplishment, Leadership subscales reflect drive and ownership orientation.

4. **VSU BARS — "Delivering Results" competency** [S3]
   Definition: "Takes ownership and accountability for own performance; Strives to consistently achieve excellence; Effectively addresses setbacks."

5. **AIHR practitioner BARS — "Initiative"** [S6]
   Level 1: "Avoids responsibility beyond assigned tasks; waits for direction."
   Level 3: "Occasionally takes initiative on familiar tasks or when prompted."
   Level 5: "Consistently takes proactive steps, anticipates needs, initiates improvements."

### Published anchor language by level

KFLA uses three levels (Unsuccessful / Successful / Exceptional); VSU uses five. The PIPE 1–5 mapping synthesizes both, with KFLA providing the behavior text and VSU providing intermediate level granularity. KFLA anchor text is quoted directly [S1].

**Level 1 (Unsuccessful)** — from KFLA "Ensures Accountability" [S1] and "Action Oriented" [S1]:
- "Fails to accept a fair share of personal responsibility."
- "Gathers little information about how things are going."
- "Is reluctant to step up to challenges; waits for someone else to take action."
- "Prefers to be one of many accountable for an assignment."

**Level 2 (Emerging/Needs Development)** — synthesized from KFLA low-end language and VSU "Delivering Results" Emerging level [S1][S3]:
- Occasionally does not meet goals or delivers commitments late.
- Is reluctant to take ownership of team performance; provides limited coaching.
- Does not consistently accept responsibility for finding solutions.

**Level 3 (Successful)** — KFLA "Ensures Accountability" Successful [S1]:
- "Follows through on commitments and makes sure others do the same."
- "Acts with a clear sense of ownership."
- "Takes personal responsibility for decisions, actions, and failures."
- "Establishes clear responsibilities and processes for monitoring work and measuring results."

**Level 4 (Distinguished)** — synthesized from KFLA Successful + VSU Distinguished for "Delivering Results" [S1][S3]:
- Consistently meets goals; follows through on personal commitments on time.
- Self-accountable for principled decisions; takes responsibility seriously.
- KFLA language: "Identifies and seizes new opportunities." "Displays a can-do attitude in good and bad times."

**Level 5 (Exceptional)** — KFLA "Ensures Accountability" Exceptional [S1]:
- "Assumes responsibility for the outcomes of others."
- "Promotes a sense of urgency and establishes and enforces individual accountability in the team."
- "Works with people to establish explicit performance standards."
- KFLA "Action Oriented" Exceptional: "Quickly and decisively takes action in fast-changing, unpredictable situations." "Shows a tremendous amount of initiative in tough situations; is exceptional at spotting and seizing opportunities."

### Big Five / NEO-PI mapping

- **Conscientiousness** (NEO-PI facets: Achievement Striving, Dutifulness, Self-Discipline, Deliberation) is the primary trait correlate [S11].
- **Hogan Prudence** (Mastery, Not Spontaneous, Avoids Trouble subscales) at the high end [S8].
- **Hogan Ambition** (Competitive, Accomplishment subscales) [S8].
- Goleman's "Initiative" competence explicitly maps to Self-Management cluster of EI [S5].

### Gap assessment

Well-sourced. Two independent validated frameworks (KFLA, VSU), plus Goleman EI and Hogan HPI personality correlates. No single-source critical claims. The AIHR practitioner anchor [S6] is corroborating rather than load-bearing.

## Competency 2: Collaboration

### PIPE's working definition

Works productively with others across roles and organizational distance; makes others more effective; creates shared understanding; handles disagreement without damaging relationships; knows when to pair vs. work solo.

### Frameworks that define it

1. **KFLA — "Collaborates" (#6) and "Communicates Effectively" (#7)** [S1]
   Collaborates: "Building partnerships and working collaboratively with others to meet shared objectives." Communicates Effectively: "Developing and delivering multi-mode communications that convey a clear understanding of the unique needs of different audiences."

2. **VSU BARS — "Collaboration" core competency (5-level)** [S3]
   Definition: "Cooperates with employees within/across departments to accomplish shared goals; treats others with dignity and respect while maintaining a friendly demeanor; acknowledges the values of others' contributions to shared goals."

3. **UC Berkeley Behavioral Anchors Matrix — "Teamwork" competency (5-level)** [S4]
   Teamwork sub-dimensions include: builds productive working relationships, cooperates and collaborates with colleagues, treats others with respect, resolves conflicts among team members, balances individual and team goals.

4. **AIHR practitioner BARS — "Teamwork"** [S6]
   Level 1: "Makes inappropriate or rude comments; shows dismissive or uncooperative behavior."
   Level 5: "Encourages open communication, helps resolve conflicts, fosters positive dynamic."

5. **Bartram Great Eight — "Supporting and Cooperating"** [S7]
   Sub-competencies: "working with people, understanding others, adapting to the team, building team spirit, recognizing and rewarding contributions, listening, consulting others." Also "Adhering to Principles & Values" (integrity sub-factor).

6. **Goleman EI framework — "Teamwork and Collaboration"** [S5]
   Listed as a Relationship Management cluster competency. Description includes emotional attunement to group dynamics, sharing credit, and building cooperative climate.

### Published anchor language by level

**Level 1 (Unsuccessful)** — VSU "Collaboration" [S3]:
- "Tends to isolate themselves from others while working toward team goals."
- "Treats other team members with hostility or indifference."
- "Talks about commitment to collaboration, but rarely demonstrates it in their actions."
- KFLA "Collaborates" Unsuccessful [S1]: "Overlooks opportunities to work collaboratively with others." "Values own interests above others'." "Prefers to work alone and be accountable for individual contributions."

**Level 2 (Emerging)** — VSU "Collaboration" Emerging [S3]:
- "Occasionally works with others to accomplish goals and tasks, although tends to complete projects individually."
- "Is occasionally resistant to others' viewpoints when analyzing a situation or developing a solution."
- "Sometimes has difficulty working well with others who have different viewpoints or perspectives."

**Level 3 (Successful)** — VSU "Collaboration" Successful [S3] and KFLA "Collaborates" Successful [S1]:
- VSU: "Consistently works with others to accomplish goals and tasks."
- VSU: "Treats all team members with a respectful, courteous, and professional manner; supports team despite different points of view or setbacks."
- VSU: "Considers the views of other people (and departments, if relevant) when analyzing a situation or developing a solution."
- KFLA: "Works cooperatively with others across the organization to achieve shared objectives." "Credits others for their contributions and accomplishments."

**Level 4 (Distinguished)** — VSU "Collaboration" Distinguished [S3] and UC Berkeley "Teamwork" Exceeds Expectations [S4]:
- VSU: "Proactively works with members of the team to complete goals." "Honors commitment to teammates and is open to incorporating others' views to resolve a situation."
- Berkeley: "Successfully builds productive, mutually beneficial relationships to solve problems and achieve common goals."
- Berkeley (conflict sub-dimension): "Manages conflict competently and efficiently; when possible, looks for and proposes middle ground solutions."

**Level 5 (Exemplary)** — KFLA "Collaborates" Exceptional [S1] and UC Berkeley "Teamwork" Exceptional [S4]:
- KFLA: "Models collaboration across the organization." "Facilitates an open dialogue with a wide variety of contributors and stakeholders." "Promotes high visibility of shared contributions to goals."
- Berkeley: "Leads colleagues to inspired cooperation and teamwork in support of department and University goals." "Models cooperation and teamwork; creates opportunities for self and others to improve working relationships."

### Big Five / NEO-PI mapping

- **Agreeableness** (NEO-PI facets: Trust, Altruism, Compliance, Tendermindedness) is primary trait correlate for Collaboration [S11].
- **Hogan Interpersonal Sensitivity** scale (Easy to Live With, Sensitive, Caring, Likes People, No Hostility subscales) at high end [S8].
- Extraversion contributes but is not necessary: collaboration is not extraversion (PIPE working definition explicitly notes "quiet ICs can be outstanding collaborators").

### Gap assessment

Strong coverage. Four independent frameworks (KFLA, VSU, UC Berkeley, Bartram Great Eight) plus personality correlates. Anchor language at all 5 levels is quoted from primary sources.

## Competency 3: Learning Orientation

### PIPE's working definition

Candidate responds to not-knowing by admitting ignorance quickly, naming specific gaps, taking concrete learning actions with outcomes, showing recent belief-updating. Contrasted with performing expertise, describing learning as passive byproduct, being unable to name what they were wrong about.

### Frameworks that define it

1. **KFLA — "Nimble Learning" (#22) and "Self-Development" (#30)** [S1]
   Nimble Learning: "Actively learning through experimentation when tackling new problems, using both successes and failures as learning fodder."
   Self-Development: "Actively seeking new ways to grow and be challenged using both formal and informal development channels."

2. **Bartram Great Eight — "Creating and Conceptualizing"** [S7]
   Sub-competency "Learning/Researching": includes "seeks learning opportunities" and "addresses challenges creatively." This factor encompasses intellectual curiosity and learning as a behavioral orientation.

3. **UC Berkeley Leadership competency — sub-dimension 3 (self-aware and open to feedback)** [S4]
   Level 1: "Not motivated to learn and grow; believes manager and University should assume responsibility for his/her development."
   Level 3: "Self-aware and open to feedback from others."
   Level 5: "Demonstrates a high degree of self-awareness; asks others for feedback on performance; is a continuous learner."

4. **Hogan HPI — Learning Approach scale and Inquisitive scale** [S8]
   Learning Approach subscales: Education (positive attitude about education), Good Memory (can remember things easily), Reading (keeps up to date). Inquisitive subscales: Curiosity (high degree of curiosity), Generates Ideas (good at generating ideas), Science Ability (takes an interest in why things happen).

5. **Goleman EI — "Adaptability" and "Achievement Drive"** [S5]
   Goleman: "Superior performers in management ranks exhibit [Adaptability]. They are open to new information and can let go of old assumptions and so adapt how they operate." Also: achievement drive as intrinsic motivation to improve.

6. **CCL Learning Agility framework** (single source — unverified for anchor text) [S9]
   CCL defines five dimensions of learning agility: Mental Agility, People Agility, Change Agility, Results Agility, and Self-Awareness. Behavioral indicators include "tries multiple approaches," "extracts lessons from failures," "enjoys the challenge of unfamiliar tasks" — language that closely parallels KFLA Nimble Learning.

### Published anchor language by level

**Level 1 (Unsuccessful)** — KFLA "Nimble Learning" Unsuccessful [S1]:
- "Struggles to learn in new situations."
- "Becomes frustrated or confused by unfamiliar tasks."
- "Gives up on new ideas too soon."
- "Resists taking a chance on untested solutions."
- KFLA "Self-Development" Unsuccessful: "Doesn't put in the effort to grow and change." "Is comfortable with current skills." "Is fearful of making mistakes that accompany development."

**Level 2 (Emerging)** — synthesized from KFLA low language and Berkeley sub-dimension [S1][S4]:
- Does not openly share expertise or information; focused largely on own current knowledge.
- Often resistant to feedback; rarely asks for help or constructive input.
- Describes learning only in terms of on-the-job exposure, not deliberate effort.

**Level 3 (Successful)** — KFLA "Nimble Learning" Successful [S1]:
- "Learns quickly when facing new situations."
- "Experiments to find new solutions."
- "Takes on the challenge of unfamiliar tasks."
- "Extracts lessons learned from failures and mistakes."
- KFLA "Self-Development" Successful: "Shows personal commitment and takes action to continuously improve." "Learns from new experiences, from others, and from structured learning."

**Level 4 (Distinguished)** — KFLA Self-Development Distinguished-analogue + Berkeley Exceeds [S1][S4]:
- KFLA: "Takes consistent action to develop new skills." "Finds ways to apply strengths to new issues." "Seeks assignments that stretch him/her beyond comfort zone."
- Berkeley: "Very self-aware; asks others for feedback in an effort to improve skills and knowledge."

**Level 5 (Exceptional)** — KFLA "Nimble Learning" Exceptional [S1]:
- "Tries multiple times using multiple methods to find the right solution."
- "Views mistakes as opportunities to learn."
- "Enjoys the challenge of unfamiliar tasks."
- "Seeks new approaches to solve problems."
- KFLA "Self-Development" Exceptional: "Is aware of the skills needed to be successful in different situations and levels." "Accepts assignments that broaden capabilities."

### Big Five / NEO-PI mapping

- **Openness to Experience** (NEO-PI facets: Ideas, Openness, Fantasy, Actions) is the primary trait correlate [S11].
- Openness to Ideas facet: "active pursuit of intellectual interests for their own sake and willingness to consider new, perhaps unconventional ideas."
- **Hogan Inquisitive** (Curiosity, Science Ability, Generates Ideas subscales) at high end [S8].
- **Hogan Learning Approach** (Education, Good Memory, Reading subscales) [S8].

### Gap assessment

Partial-strong coverage. No single peer-reviewed BARS validation study found with 5-point numbered anchor text specific to learning orientation as a work competency. The CCL learning agility framework is frequently cited but anchor text was inaccessible (PDF binary). KFLA Nimble Learning and Self-Development together provide strong 3-level anchor coverage for the translation. Mark as: well-sourced for behavioral language, gap exists for validated level-scale reliability data.

## Competency 4: Conflict Handling

### PIPE's working definition

Navigates disagreement, friction, and hard conversations; engages directly and early; separates person from problem; changes position when persuaded; repairs relationships after conflict. Contrasted with avoidance, passive aggression, escalation, grudge-holding, or framing disagreement as personal attack.

### Frameworks that define it

1. **KFLA — "Manages Conflict" (#9)** [S1]
   Definition: "Handling conflict situations effectively, with a minimum of noise."

2. **UC Berkeley Behavioral Anchors — "Teamwork" sub-dimension 4 (Resolves conflicts)** [S4]
   Full 5-level anchor set extracted (see below).

3. **Goleman EI — "Conflict Management" (Relationship Management cluster)** [S5]
   "A talent of those skilled in the Conflict Management competence is spotting trouble as it is brewing and taking steps to calm those involved. Here the arts of listening and empathizing are crucial to the skills of handling difficult people and situations with diplomacy, encouraging debate and open discussion, and orchestrating win-win situations."

4. **AIHR practitioner BARS — conflict management (nursing context)** [S6]
   Level 1 (Unsatisfactory): "Displays frustration and impatience with difficult patients and usually requires assistance."
   Level 2 (Needs Improvement): "Sometimes displays frustration but can usually manage conflicts without assistance."
   Level 3 (Satisfactory): "Interacts with patients in an empathetic manner without indications of impatience."
   Level 4 (Exceeds Expectations): "Shows empathy and supports other staff in managing difficult patients."
   Level 5 (Excellent): "Shows empathy and regularly provides assistance to others with difficult patients."
   *(Note: nursing-context anchors; useful for structural reference, not for software engineer candidate interviews directly.)*

5. **Bartram Great Eight — "Supporting and Cooperating" sub-factor "Adhering to Principles & Values"** [S7]
   Includes "handling criticism" and "coping with pressure" in the Adapting & Coping factor, which overlaps with de-escalation.

6. **VSU BARS — "Collaboration" sub-behaviors around conflict initiation** [S3]
   Unsuccessful: "Usually waits for others to solve interpersonal conflicts and problems."
   Successful: "Regularly initiates communication to help solve interpersonal conflicts and problems."
   Distinguished: "Communicates in a manner that helps resolve interpersonal conflict and problems."

### Published anchor language by level

**Level 1 (Unsuccessful)** — KFLA "Manages Conflict" Unsuccessful [S1]:
- "Avoids conflict."
- "Struggles to make progress when working through disagreements."
- "Takes sides without digging deeply enough to fully understand the issues."
- "Allows conflicts to cause massive disruption in the organization."
- "Puts people on the defensive."
- "May accommodate, wanting everyone to get along."

**Level 2 (Emerging)** — synthesized from KFLA and VSU language [S1][S3]:
- Occasionally initiates communication to resolve problems within the team, but often waits for others to act.
- Describes conflicts in ways that paint the other party unsympathetically; limited evidence of steelmanning.
- Outcomes described without the process; unclear what specific actions were taken.

**Level 3 (Successful)** — KFLA "Manages Conflict" Successful [S1]:
- "Steps up to conflicts, seeing them as opportunities."
- "Works out tough agreements and settles disputes equitably."
- "Facilitates breakthroughs by integrating diverse views and finding common ground or acceptable alternatives."
- "Settles differences in productive ways with minimum noise."

**Level 4 (Distinguished)** — UC Berkeley "Teamwork" conflict sub-dimension, Exceeds Expectations [S4]:
- "Manages conflict competently and efficiently; when possible, looks for and proposes middle ground solutions."
- VSU Distinguished: "Communicates in a manner that helps resolve interpersonal conflict and problems."

**Level 5 (Exceptional)** — KFLA "Manages Conflict" Exceptional [S1]:
- "Anticipates conflicts before they happen, based on knowledge of interpersonal and group dynamics."
- "Asks questions and listens closely to all issues presented by stakeholders."
- "Finds common ground and drives to consensus, ensuring that all feel heard."
- "Defuses high-tension situations effectively."
- Berkeley Exceptional: "Skillfully and proactively addresses conflict; seeks and achieves 'win-win' resolutions."

### Big Five / NEO-PI mapping

- **Agreeableness** (NEO-PI facets: Compliance, Tendermindedness, Trust): moderate-high agreeableness with non-submissive compliance facet (willingness to engage, not just yield) [S11].
- **Low Neuroticism / Adjustment** (Hogan: Calmness, Even-Tempered, No Hostility subscales) at high end [S8].
- **Hogan Interpersonal Sensitivity** (No Hostility: "Generally accepting" vs. "Critical of others") [S8].
- Note: PIPE's definition explicitly rejects "being calm" as sufficient (calmness without engagement is avoidance) — the Agreeableness mapping must be calibrated against Assertiveness facet to avoid false positives for avoidant candidates.

### Gap assessment

Strong coverage. Three independent frameworks (KFLA, UC Berkeley, Goleman EI) with direct anchor language plus VSU sub-behavior support. The AIHR nursing BARS is structurally useful as a cross-context validation of the 5-level structure. No peer-reviewed IO psychology BARS validation study specific to conflict-handling as a work competency was found — mark as well-sourced practitioner coverage, unvalidated reliability.

## Competency 5: Self-Awareness

### PIPE's working definition

Sees themselves accurately — strengths, weaknesses, and blind spots; names real weaknesses with concrete examples of them costing something; describes how their style lands on specific others; recognizes patterns across multiple past situations; distinguishes "what I did" from "what I wish I had done." Contrasted with disguised-strength weaknesses, no genuine vulnerability, attributing all difficulty to others.

### Frameworks that define it

1. **KFLA — "Demonstrates Self-Awareness" (#29)** [S1]
   Definition: "Using a combination of feedback and reflection to gain productive insight into personal strengths and weaknesses."

2. **Goleman/Boyatzis ECI — "Accurate Self-Assessment" and "Emotional Self-Awareness"** [S5]
   Boyatzis (1982, cited in Goleman 2001): "Individuals with the Accurate Self-Assessment competence are aware of their abilities and limitations, seek out feedback and learn from their mistakes, and know where they need to improve and when to work with others who have complementary strengths. Accurate Self-Assessment was the competence found in virtually every 'star performer' in a study of several hundred knowledge workers." Star performers: "on 360-degree competence assessments, average performers typically overestimate their strengths, whereas star performers rarely do."

3. **Tasha Eurich research — internal vs. external self-awareness (practitioner-academic)** [S10]
   Eurich (organizational psychologist, cited in multiple practitioner sources): defines two independent components: internal self-awareness (how clearly we see our own values, passions, reactions) and external self-awareness (how accurately we understand how others perceive us). Key finding: these do not naturally co-occur. Observable behavioral indicators include: high internal/low external — "confident shares 'honest feedback' without noticing others shutting down"; high external/low internal — "brilliant at reading rooms but unable to make decisions because lost connection with own preferences."

4. **UC Berkeley Leadership competency — sub-dimension 3 "Self-aware and open to feedback"** [S4]
   Level 1: "Not motivated to learn and grow; believes manager and University should assume responsibility for his/her development."
   Level 3: "Self-aware and open to feedback from others."
   Level 5: "Demonstrates a high degree of self-awareness; asks others for feedback on performance; is a continuous learner."

5. **Hogan HPI — Adjustment scale (multiple subscales)** [S8]
   Adjustment subscales: Empathy (absence of irritability — "Irritated by others' faults" vs. "Empathic"), No Guilt (absence of regret — "Prone to worry about past mistakes" vs. "Does not worry about past mistakes"), Good Attachment ("Hostile toward authority" vs. "Positive attitude toward authority"). High Adjustment + high Prudence (Not Autonomous subscale: "Concerned about how others view him/her") is a behavioral marker of other-awareness.

6. **VSU BARS — Collaboration + Communication** (overlap: self-awareness regarding one's own impact on team) [S3]
   Successful: "Treats all team members with a respectful, courteous, and professional manner; supports team despite different points of view or setbacks" (implies awareness of impact).

### Published anchor language by level

**Level 1 (Unsuccessful)** — KFLA "Demonstrates Self-Awareness" Unsuccessful [S1]:
- "Doesn't reflect on past mistakes."
- "Acts defensively when given feedback."
- "Is unaware of own skills and interpersonal impact."
- "Makes excuses and blames others."

**Level 2 (Emerging)** — synthesized from KFLA and Berkeley language [S1][S4]:
- Often resistant to feedback; rarely asks for help or seeks external perspective.
- Names a "weakness" that is mild, framed to soften, or is actually a strength in disguise.
- No evidence of connecting one's own behavior to outcomes for others.

**Level 3 (Successful)** — KFLA "Demonstrates Self-Awareness" Successful [S1]:
- "Reflects on activities and impact on others."
- "Proactively seeks feedback without being defensive."
- "Is open to criticism and talking about shortcomings."
- "Admits mistakes and gains insight from experiences."
- "Knows strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and limits."

**Level 4 (Distinguished)** — Berkeley "Leadership" sub-dimension 3 Exceeds Expectations [S4]:
- "Very self-aware; asks others for feedback in an effort to improve skills and knowledge."
- Goleman/Boyatzis "Accurate Self-Assessment": aware of abilities and limitations; seeks feedback and learns from mistakes; knows where they need to improve and when to work with others who have complementary strengths [S5].

**Level 5 (Exceptional)** — KFLA "Demonstrates Self-Awareness" Exceptional [S1]:
- "Seeks and acts on feedback from a wide variety of people."
- "Willingly admits and takes responsibility for mistakes and shortcomings."
- "Views criticism as helpful."
- "Can articulate the causes behind own feelings and moods."
- Berkeley Exceptional: "Demonstrates a high degree of self-awareness; asks others for feedback on performance; is a continuous learner." [S4]

### Big Five / NEO-PI mapping

- **Low Neuroticism** (NEO-PI): the Adjustment scale captures emotional stability which enables self-reflection without defensiveness. But note: high Neuroticism does not preclude self-awareness — it can coexist with excessive self-focus [S11].
- **Openness to Experience** (Introspection-related facets: Fantasy, Feelings) correlates with internal self-awareness [S11].
- **Conscientiousness** (Deliberation facet): "careful consideration of options before acting" relates to reflective self-assessment [S11].
- **Hogan Adjustment** — Empathy, Trusting, Good Attachment subscales at high end; No Guilt at moderate level (excessive guilt ≠ self-awareness) [S8].
- Eurich (2018) caution: the two components of self-awareness are independent. A scale should separately probe "do you see yourself accurately?" from "do you understand your impact on others?" [S10] (single source — unverified independently).

### Gap assessment

Partial coverage. KFLA provides direct 3-level language. Goleman/Boyatzis provides validation evidence (Accurate Self-Assessment predicts star performance across hundreds of managers — Boyatzis 1982). Eurich provides a conceptual two-factor model useful for avoiding false positives, but no numbered BARS instrument was found. Berkeley sub-dimension provides 5-level structure but thin anchor text per level. This is the competency with the most derivation required from practitioner sources — mark accordingly.

The practitioner derivation recommendation for the two gap competencies: use Korn Ferry's three-level language as anchors for levels 1, 3, 5, then construct levels 2 and 4 by interpolating observable behaviors that are behaviorally intermediate — "sometimes exhibits," "inconsistently demonstrates," or "emerging but not consistent" phrasings consistent with VSU's Emerging and Distinguished level language patterns.
